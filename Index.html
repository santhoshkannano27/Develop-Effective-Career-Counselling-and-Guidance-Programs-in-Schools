02 — Why It Matters

Most students choose a stream before they've explored a single career.

Career decisions made under pressure, on incomplete information, tend to get revisited expensively — later, and at higher cost to the student.

A

Decisions made too early

Subject streams are often locked in around age 14–15, well before students have any real exposure to what different careers involve day to day.

B

Guidance without structure

Where counselling exists, it's often a single session, reactive rather than planned, and disconnected from the rest of the curriculum.

C

Mismatch and dropout risk

Poorly informed choices show up later as course dropouts, career changes, and disengagement — costs that a structured program is built to prevent.

03 — The Route

Five waypoints, one continuous journey.

Each pillar builds on the last. A student who skips a waypoint arrives at graduation with a plan built on guesswork.

Discover Self-assessment

Help students understand their own aptitudes, interests and values before any career talk begins.

  • Psychometric & aptitude testing — interest inventories, personality and ability assessments run by trained counsellors.
  • Reflective exercises — journals and structured worksheets on strengths, subjects enjoyed, and out-of-school activities.
  • One-on-one intake sessions — a baseline conversation logged for every student entering Grade 8 or 9.

Explore Career exposure

Turn abstract career names into first-hand experience through people, places, and information.

  • Career talks & alumni panels — professionals from varied fields visiting each term.
  • Job shadowing & site visits — short placements with local employers during breaks.
  • Career fairs — an annual event connecting students with colleges, industries, and training bodies.

Plan Individual pathway mapping

Convert exploration into a written, individual plan owned by the student, not the counsellor.

  • Individual Career Plan (ICP) — a living document reviewed with a counsellor twice a year.
  • Subject & stream mapping — aligning elective choices to the pathways a student is genuinely considering.
  • Goal-setting sessions — short-term (this year), medium-term (post-school), and long-term milestones.

Build Skill & readiness development

Layer in the practical skills a plan needs to survive contact with the real world.

  • Workshops — resume writing, interview practice, and communication skills from Grade 10 onward.
  • Mentorship — pairing senior students or alumni with juniors in related interest areas.
  • Internships & projects — short structured placements or capstone projects tied to a chosen field.

Sustain Follow-up & transition

Guidance doesn't end at graduation — the program tracks students into their next step and back into the system.

  • College & application support — help with applications, scholarships, and entrance exams.
  • Post-graduation follow-up — a one-year check-in survey to see how choices played out.
  • Feedback into the program — alumni outcomes feed back into next year's Discover phase.
04 — Rollout

Phased over one academic year.

Programs that launch everything at once tend to overload counsellors and confuse students. Phasing keeps each layer working before adding the next.

PhaseTimingFocus
Phase 1 — Foundation Term 1 Appoint or train counsellors, audit existing resources, run baseline self-assessments for target grades.
Phase 2 — Exposure Term 1–2 Launch career talks, alumni panels, and the first career fair; begin building an employer & college contact list.
Phase 3 — Planning Term 2 Roll out Individual Career Plans, align them with subject and stream selection windows.
Phase 4 — Review Term 3 Run skill workshops, collect outcome data, and review the whole cycle with staff and student feedback.
05 — Who Carries It

No single office runs this alone.

A guidance program survives on shared ownership — each group holds a piece the others can't provide.

School counsellors

Own the day-to-day: assessments, one-on-ones, Individual Career Plans, and coordinating the other three groups.

Teachers

Fold career context into everyday subjects and flag students who need extra guidance early.

Parents

Bring home context, reinforce plans made at school, and take part in review conversations each term.

Employers & colleges

Supply real exposure — talks, site visits, internships — and a channel into what comes after school.

07 — Rough Terrain

What tends to go wrong.

Naming the failure modes in advance is cheaper than discovering them mid-year.

Obstacle

Too few trained counsellors

Address with tiered support — train a small group of teachers as guidance facilitators, reserving trained counsellors for one-on-one and complex cases.

Obstacle

Low parent involvement

Address with short, scheduled review meetings tied to existing parent-teacher days, rather than separate optional sessions.

Obstacle

Weak employer network

Address by starting with alumni — they convert into talks, shadowing hosts, and internship offers faster than cold outreach.

Obstacle

No budget line

Address by sequencing low-cost elements first — talks and self-assessment — and using early outcomes to justify funding fairs and workshops.
